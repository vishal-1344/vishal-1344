I am a computational researcher focusing on time-domain astrophysics, simulation-based inference (SBI), and physics-informed dynamical modeling.

My methodological edge in signal processing stems from an unconventional but deeply empirical background: years of active work as a digital music producer and live DJ. My workflow in sound design and audio engineering has always been rigorously analytical and mathematically precise. Thousands of hours spent manipulating complex acoustic dynamics forged an intuitive grasp of the frequency domain — sculpting parametric equalizers (purely a continuous Fourier and biquad transfer mechanism), manipulating phase coherency to eliminate destructive comb filtering, and driving non-linear saturation to generate harmonic series. Furthermore, navigating the sub-millisecond granularity of digital audio workstations (DAWs) trained me to isolate micro-temporal transients and spectral leakage by ear long before I formalized these non-stationary phenomena through discrete-time mathematics.

Today, I translate that empirical intuition to the streaming pipelines of next-generation observatories. Whether deploying amortized simulation-based inference (SBI) on gravitational-wave strain (LIGO/Virgo/LISA) or correcting interstellar plasma dispersion for real-time radio transient alerts (FRBs and pulsars), the underlying physics mirrors acoustic transfer function deconvolution, adaptive filtering, and non-stationary spectral estimation.

In machine learning, my research targets autonomous, adaptive computation for dynamical systems. Moving beyond static feedforward architectures, I design closed-loop, metacognitive neural frameworks (such as SCI) that treat inference as an active dynamical process with continuous state feedback and Lyapunov-bounded stability. Ultimately, I build physics-informed, self-regulating models engineered to operate reliably within the extreme low-SNR and non-stationary realities of space telemetry.
